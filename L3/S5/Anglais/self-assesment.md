# The Presentation

## Content: Satisfactory

My content was organized with a lot of cited information, and I explained my reasoning to arrive at these results.

## Comprehensibility: Needs work

My content was too dense; there was too much information to follow my presentation easily. After a while, people no longer knew what I was talking about. There were a lot of numbers that were more or less explained, but the reasoning was too weak. I should have removed some content to make it clearer.

My intro was good, but the rest leaves something to be desired.

## Pronunciation: Needs work

I made a lot of pauses and my speech was jerky. To manage to say everything, I had to speed up during the parts where I spoke, which meant that people couldn't really understand what I was trying to say.

No part had a consistent flow.

However, my pronunciation seemed not too bad.

## Grammar: Needs work

A lot of repetition, tenses were only partially correct, and sentence structures were poor.

## Eye Contact: Satisfactory

I alternated my gaze between my PowerPoint to read the numbers, my watch to check the time, and looking at the audience the rest of the time (about 60% on the audience). Despite having my note sheet in hand, I didn't use it.

## Gestures: Satisfactory

I used my hands to present my numbers, though it would have been better if I hadn't been holding my note sheets.
Sometimes I didn't know where to stand so I wouldn't block the PowerPoint, and my posture wasn't upright enough.

## PowerPoint: Very good

All the important information was on the slides without overwhelming the audience, and the colors had good contrast to make reading easy for the public.

## Evaluation Stage:

1. It is very useful because when you give your presentation, you cannot think about everything you are doing. Looking back in review gives us plenty of time to see what I can improve, just like a coach.

2. I am going to improve my presentation a lot because I made a lot of errors.

3. My PowerPoint was good.
