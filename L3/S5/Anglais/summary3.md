# Being multilingual benefits the brain.

## Definition :

dementia = progressive loss of memory and cognitive abilities, especially in old age
fortnight = a period of two weeks
stroke = a serious medical condition caused by interrupted blood supply to the brain

## Key words and sentences :

- 60 to 75% of the world population speaks at least two languages
- being monolingual means being in the minority and missing out
- South Africa has 11 official languages, Australia has over 130 indigenous languages
- super languages (English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic) are increasingly expected
- speaking multiple languages brings cognitive benefits: better concentration and problem-solving
- medical benefits: faster stroke recovery and delayed onset of dementia
- languages evolve through social pressures (trade, travel), unlike biological evolution

- to keep the benefits of bilingualism, you must practice your languages regularly

## Abstract :

60 to 75% of the world population speaks at least two languages, so being monolingual means being in the minority and missing out. For example, South Africa has 11 official languages, Australia has over 130 indigenous languages, and super languages (English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic) are increasingly expected. Furthermore, speaking multiple languages brings cognitive benefits: better concentration and problem-solving, as well as medical benefits: faster stroke recovery and delayed onset of dementia. Historically, languages evolve through social pressures (trade, travel), unlike biological evolution. However, we lose one language every fortnight; half will be extinct by the end of the century. Therefore, to keep the benefits of bilingualism, you must practice your languages regularly.

## Opinion :

From my point of view, multilingualism brings much more than just intellectual benefits. Knowing a foreign language makes it easier to connect with others and adapt to new environments. Moreover, it helps us better understand people by discovering their culture through the prism of their language. Finally, it fosters greater open-mindedness. As Federico Mayor once said: 'Culture is an instrument of peace, because it allows people to understand one another beyond their differences.'
